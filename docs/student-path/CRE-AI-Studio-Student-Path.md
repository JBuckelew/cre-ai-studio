# The CRE AI Practicum

### A university-sponsored build program by CRE AI Studio

*Program design document — v1, August 2026*

> **Alternate program names for consideration:** The CRE AI Practicum (recommended) · The Student Studio · CRE AI Lab: Campus Edition

---

## The one-page summary

**What it is.** A semester-aligned, 12-week hands-on build program that runs alongside a university's existing real estate curriculum. Students don't study AI — they build with it, every week, on real deal documents, with the same tools and community used by working professionals at firms like CBRE, Newmark, MetLife, Colliers, and Cushman & Wakefield. The program culminates in the **CRE AI Studio "AI for CRE" Certification** — a proctored, two-part exam (knowledge test + screen-recorded agent build) that students add directly to LinkedIn.

**Who pays.** The university — never the student. The Practicum is licensed by the school as a program benefit, the way schools sponsor Bloomberg terminal access, ARGUS training, or CFA prep. Students get it free with their enrollment.

**What it is not.** It is not a course, and it does not compete with one. A university's CRE AI course teaches students *about* artificial intelligence in real estate — the concepts, the landscape, the implications. The Practicum is the lab layer underneath it: the place where students put hands on the tools, ship working artifacts, and leave with a portfolio and a credential. The two are designed to stack.

**Why schools sponsor it.** Because "our students graduate AI-certified, with a portfolio of working AI tools they built themselves" is a recruiting and placement story no lecture can produce — delivered with zero faculty lift, zero admin burden, and semester-end reporting on engagement and certification pass rates.

## 1. The gap this fills

Real estate programs have moved fast on AI. Many — including the best ones — now offer a CRE AI course. Those courses do what courses do well: frameworks, case studies, critical thinking about where the industry is going.

But there are four things a semester course structurally cannot do, no matter how good the professor:

1. **Stay current.** Syllabi are locked months in advance. The AI toolscape changes weekly — models, features, and best practices from even one semester ago are stale. The Practicum's content is produced continuously by a team whose full-time job is teaching AI to CRE professionals, and includes a weekly "AI This Week in CRE" briefing.

2. **Give every student reps.** A course has lectures, readings, maybe a group project. The Practicum is one build lab per week, every week — each student personally building rent roll analyzers, lease abstraction workflows, underwriting copilots, and autonomous agents. Skills come from reps, not slides.

3. **Put students in the room with the industry.** Practicum students join the same private community where hundreds of working CRE professionals — brokers, asset managers, analysts, attorneys from the industry's biggest firms — ask questions, share workflows, and post what's working. That's not a guest-speaker cameo; it's a semester of ambient mentorship and networking.

4. **Issue a credential recruiters can verify.** A grade in a course says a student passed a course. The AI for CRE Certification says a student sat down, on camera, and built a working CRE AI agent from a cold brief — and passed a knowledge exam covering the full applied curriculum. It lives on their LinkedIn profile under Licenses & Certifications, issued by CRE AI Studio.

The result: the classroom teaches judgment, the Practicum builds capability. Schools shouldn't have to pick — and with this model, they don't.

## 2. Classroom vs. Practicum — designed to stack

| | The university's CRE AI course | The CRE AI Practicum |
|---|---|---|
| **Teaches** | Concepts, landscape, implications | Tools, workflows, building |
| **Format** | Lectures, readings, cases | Weekly live build labs + async lessons |
| **Materials** | Fixed at semester start | Updated continuously; weekly AI briefings |
| **Output** | Papers, exams, a grade | Working AI tools, a portfolio, a certification |
| **Taught by** | Faculty | Operators who train CRE professionals full-time |
| **Network** | Classmates | Hundreds of working CRE professionals |
| **Cost to student** | Tuition | $0 — sponsored by the school |

Faculty stay in control: the Practicum maps to the academic calendar, and faculty get full visibility into the curriculum and their cohort's progress. Where a professor wants tighter integration — e.g., accepting Practicum artifacts as course deliverables, or a co-taught session — we build that with them.

## 3. Program structure — the 12-week arc

One semester. Three phases. Every week: one live build session (recorded, posted within 24 hours), async step-by-step video lessons, open office hours, a community build challenge, and the "AI This Week in CRE" briefing.

### Phase 1 — Foundations in Practice (Weeks 1–3)

*Goal: every student leaves week 3 with a personal AI workspace and real fluency in the core tools.*

- **Week 1 — Your AI Workspace.** Set up and configure ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini; how large language models actually behave (and fail); first working session analyzing a real offering memorandum.
- **Week 2 — Prompting for CRE.** Prompt patterns that matter in practice; the CRE Prompt Library; hands-on document analysis of rent rolls and leases.
- **Week 3 — AI-Powered Research.** Market research and comp gathering with AI; verification habits and hallucination control — the professional discipline of trusting-but-checking model output.

### Phase 2 — Build Labs (Weeks 4–9)

*Goal: six weeks, six shipped artifacts. Every lab produces something a student can show a recruiter.*

- **Week 4 — Rent Roll & T12 Analysis Lab.** Turn messy property financials into clean analysis: occupancy, loss-to-lease, expense anomalies.
- **Week 5 — Lease Abstraction Lab.** Build a repeatable AI workflow that abstracts commercial leases into structured summaries.
- **Week 6 — Market Research Agent Lab.** Build an agent that gathers, synthesizes, and cites market data for a submarket.
- **Week 7 — Underwriting Copilot Lab.** From offering memorandum to draft investment memo: assumptions, returns framing, risks.
- **Week 8 — Automation Lab.** Connect AI to real workflows with Zapier / Make / n8n — intake, extraction, notification pipelines.
- **Week 9 — Pitch & Content Lab.** AI-generated decks and polished deliverables: turn the week-7 memo into a presentation-grade pitch.

### Phase 3 — Certification (Weeks 10–12)

*Goal: convert a semester of building into a verified, LinkedIn-ready credential.*

- **Week 10 — Agent Build Review.** Students rebuild and harden their strongest agent; mock practical against a sample brief.
- **Week 11 — Exam Prep.** Structured review of the full curriculum; timed practice exam; dedicated office hours.
- **Week 12 — Certification Window.** Students sit the two-part AI for CRE Certification exam (detailed below).

## 4. The "AI for CRE" Certification

The credential is the spine of the program — the thing a student walks away with that a course grade can't replicate.

**Part 1 — Knowledge exam.** Multiple choice, covering the full applied curriculum: LLM behavior and limitations, CRE prompting, document analysis, research verification, automation architecture, and agent design. Timed, online, closed-book.

**Part 2 — Practical exam.** The differentiator. Each student receives a cold brief (e.g., "build an agent that abstracts this lease and flags non-standard clauses") and **screen-records themselves building a working CRE AI agent** against it. Submissions are graded on a published rubric: does it work, is the approach sound, does the student demonstrate command of the tools — not just the ability to follow along.

**Passing both parts earns the CRE AI Studio "AI for CRE" Certification:**

- A verified credential added to LinkedIn under **Licenses & Certifications**, issued by CRE AI Studio
- A shareable certificate (co-brandable with the sponsoring university)
- Listing in the Studio's certified-student directory that hiring member firms can browse

**Retake policy.** One included retake per part. Students who don't pass by semester end can complete certification in a following exam window.

**Why it lands with recruiters.** Every resume this decade will say "familiar with AI tools." A screen-recorded, rubric-graded build is proof, not a claim. That's the difference between listing a skill and demonstrating one — and it's a story the school gets to tell about its graduates.

## 5. What makes it feel nothing like class

- **Portfolio over grades.** Six shipped artifacts plus a certified agent build. Every week ends with something real.
- **A real credential.** The certification goes on LinkedIn; a problem set doesn't.
- **The industry is in the room.** Students join the working-professional community from day one — same channels, same conversations, same office hours. Mentor matching connects students with member professionals in the lane they want to work in (brokerage, asset management, development, legal).
- **Taught by operators.** The instructors run an AI training company for CRE professionals — the same lessons Fortune-500-adjacent CRE teams pay for, tuned for students.
- **Career edge, explicitly.** The final weeks include positioning help: how to present the certification and portfolio on a resume, in interviews, and on LinkedIn as "the analyst who can actually build with AI."

## 6. The sponsorship model

Students never pay. The university licenses the Practicum the way it licenses ARGUS training or Bloomberg terminals — as infrastructure for student outcomes. Three ways in (all pricing illustrative and open to structuring):

| | **Pilot Cohort** | **Department License** | **Campus License** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | One semester, capped seats | Per-seat, per semester | Annual, program-wide |
| **Seats** | Up to 50 students | 25+ seats | All enrolled RE students |
| **Indicative pricing** | Flat fee (~$7,500) | ~$125–150 / student / semester, volume-tiered | Annual flat license, custom |
| **Includes** | Full program + certification | Everything in Pilot + faculty reporting dashboard + one guest lecture per semester | Everything in Department + co-branded certification + curriculum previews + standing faculty access |
| **Best for** | Proving it out this semester | A single graduate or undergrad program | Schools making AI fluency a program-wide differentiator |

**Every tier includes, for the school:**

- **Zero lift.** Studio runs everything — onboarding, sessions, community, office hours, exams, credentialing.
- **Semester-end reporting.** Enrollment, engagement, completion, and certification pass rates for the school's cohort.
- **Faculty access.** Instructors see the full curriculum and materials, and can sit in on any session.
- **A marketing story.** "Our students graduate AI-certified" — usable in program marketing, rankings narratives, and employer conversations.

**The pipeline logic (why this is sustainable for Studio):** certified students graduate into the industry already fluent in the Studio ecosystem, and convert to professional membership at an alumni rate — so the sponsored price can stay far below retail while the program stays first-class.

## 7. Outcomes & success metrics

What we report to the sponsoring school each semester, and what "working" looks like:

- **Completion rate** — share of enrolled students active through week 12 (target: 70%+)
- **Certification pass rate** — first-sit and final (target: 60%+ first-sit, 80%+ final)
- **Artifacts shipped** — average build-lab artifacts completed per student (target: 5 of 6)
- **Placement signal** — certified students reporting the credential/portfolio surfaced in interviews (tracked via exit survey)
- **Student NPS** — end-of-semester (target: 50+)
